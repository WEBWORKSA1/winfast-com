Sweepstakes vs Contests vs Lotteries: What's the Difference?

Prize, chance and consideration explained — why sweepstakes are free, why contests are judged on skill, and why lotteries are regulated.

By the WinFast.com Editorial Team · Reviewed against official sources · Editorial policy

The words get used interchangeably, but legally they are very different. Understanding the difference protects you as an entrant and keeps businesses on the right side of the law.

The three elements

A promotion that combines all three of the following is generally treated as a lottery, which in most places only governments or licensed charities may run:

ElementMeaning
PrizeSomething of value is awarded.
ChanceThe winner is picked at random rather than on merit.
ConsiderationEntrants must pay or give something of value (often a purchase) to enter.

Legal promotions remove one element:

  • Sweepstakes remove consideration. They are free to enter — “no purchase necessary” — even if a purchase also gets you an entry, there must be an equal free route.
  • Contests remove chance. Winners are chosen by judges against published criteria (creativity, accuracy, skill).
  • Giveaways is an informal word; legally most giveaways are sweepstakes.

Canada's skill-testing question

Under Canada's Criminal Code, a promotion awarded purely by chance can be treated as an illegal lottery. That is why Canadian winners must usually answer a skill-testing question before receiving a prize. Quebec adds its own rules administered by the Régie des alcools, des courses et des jeux.

What this means for you

  • If a “sweepstakes” requires payment with no free alternative, be cautious — it may be an unlawful lottery or a scam.
  • Contests reward effort: study the judging criteria and past winners.
  • Lottery tickets, betting and casino-style “sweepstakes casinos” are gambling products. WinFast.com does not list them.
This guide is general information, not legal advice. Promotion law varies by country, state and province.
