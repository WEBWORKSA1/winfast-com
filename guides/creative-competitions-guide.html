How to Win Writing, Photo and Design Competitions

Judged competitions reward strategy as much as talent. How to read the brief, match the judges, and protect your rights.

By the WinFast.com Editorial Team · Reviewed against official sources · Editorial policy

Judged competitions have far fewer entrants than random draws, and your result is in your control. The best creatives still lose when they ignore the brief.

1. Treat the brief as the judging sheet

Highlight every requirement: theme, word or file limits, format, naming rules, deadlines in the organiser's time zone. Entries that break a technical rule are often discarded before judging.

2. Study past winners

Past winners reveal what judges actually reward: tone, risk-taking, technical polish, or storytelling. Don't copy — calibrate.

3. Lead with a strong first impression

  • Writing: the first paragraph decides whether a tired reader continues.
  • Photography: the thumbnail must work at small size.
  • Design: the problem and solution must be readable in five seconds.

4. Read the rights clause

Some competitions take broad, perpetual rights to every entry (“rights grabs”). Reputable ones take a limited licence to promote winners. If the rights clause is broader than the prize is worth, skip it.

5. Weigh entry fees

Fees are common in prestigious awards. Compare fee vs. prize vs. number of entrants vs. career value. Many top programmes have free categories or fee waivers.

6. Submit early

Servers crash on deadline day. Submit 48 hours early and keep a confirmation.
