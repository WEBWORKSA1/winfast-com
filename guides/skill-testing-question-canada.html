The Canadian Skill-Testing Question, Explained

Why Canadian contest winners answer a math question, how it works, and what Quebec residents should know.

By the WinFast.com Editorial Team · Reviewed against official sources · Editorial policy

If you have ever won a prize in Canada, you were probably asked something like “(12 × 4) + 30 ÷ 5 − 7 = ?”. This isn't a formality invented by sponsors — it exists because of how Canadian law treats games of chance.

Why it exists

Canada's Criminal Code restricts lotteries and schemes that award prizes purely by chance. Adding an element of skill — a mathematical question answered without assistance — is the standard way promoters ensure a promotional contest is not considered a lottery. In practice, the draw selects a potential winner, who becomes the actual winner only after answering correctly.

How to answer correctly

  • Use the order of operations: brackets, multiplication and division (left to right), then addition and subtraction (left to right).
  • Usually you must answer without a calculator and within a time limit — read the instructions.
  • A wrong answer can forfeit the prize, so slow down.

Example: (12 × 4) + 30 ÷ 5 − 7 → 48 + 6 − 7 = 47.

Other Canadian rules entrants should know

  • The Competition Act requires adequate disclosure of the number and value of prizes, regional allocation, odds-affecting facts and how to enter.
  • Many national contests exclude or treat Quebec separately, because Quebec has additional rules administered by the Régie des alcools, des courses et des jeux.
  • No purchase can be required — there is usually a free “no purchase necessary” entry method.
General information only — not legal advice.
