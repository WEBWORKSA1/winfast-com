How to Win a Hackathon: A Weekend Playbook

From picking the right hackathon to scoping, demo-driven building and a winning pitch — a repeatable playbook for hackathon prizes.

By the WinFast.com Editorial Team · Reviewed against official sources · Editorial policy

Hackathons are one of the best risk-reward bets for builders: free to enter, prizes in cash and credits, and a portfolio piece either way. Winning is rarely about the best code — it is about the best demo of a clear idea that fits the judging criteria.

1. Pick the right hackathon

  • Read the prize list. Sponsor-specific prizes (“best use of X API”) often get far fewer submissions than the grand prize.
  • Check team size limits and whether you can bring prior work.
  • Online hackathons with long submission windows reward polish; in-person weekends reward speed.

2. Reverse-engineer the judging criteria

Most hackathons publish criteria such as impact, technical difficulty, creativity, design and use of sponsor tools. Score your idea against each one before you write a line of code. If a criterion isn't visible in your demo, it doesn't exist for the judges.

3. Scope ruthlessly

Build one “wow” path end-to-end. Fake what you must (hard-coded data, mocked services) and be honest about it. A working narrow demo beats an ambitious broken one every time.

4. Build the demo first

  1. Hour 1–2: problem statement, user, and the 60-second demo script.
  2. Then build exactly what the script needs.
  3. Stop feature work with at least 20% of the time left for polish, video and write-up.

5. Win the pitch

  • Open with the problem in one sentence and who has it.
  • Show, don't tell — go straight into the live demo.
  • Close with impact and what's next.
  • For online hackathons, the 2–3 minute video is your pitch. Script it, record it cleanly, add captions.

6. Submit like a pro

Clear title, screenshots, architecture notes, a link to the repository, and every sponsor prize you qualify for selected. Many teams lose prizes they were eligible for simply because they did not opt in.
