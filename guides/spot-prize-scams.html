How to Spot Prize Scams and Fake Giveaways

Red flags of fake 'you've won' messages, cloned social giveaways and prize-fee scams — and what to do if you are targeted.

By the WinFast.com Editorial Team · Reviewed against official sources · Editorial policy

Wherever there are real prizes, there are fake ones. Scammers copy brand logos, clone social accounts and send convincing “congratulations” messages. Here is how to tell the difference in seconds.

Seven red flags

  1. You must pay to claim. Fees, “taxes”, shipping or processing charges paid to the sponsor are a scam signal. Legitimate sponsors never require payment to release a prize.
  2. Gift cards or crypto. Requests to pay with gift cards, wire transfers or cryptocurrency are a near-certain scam.
  3. You never entered. You cannot win a promotion you did not enter.
  4. Urgency and secrecy. “Respond within 1 hour” or “don't tell anyone” is pressure, not policy.
  5. Look-alike accounts. A DM from “@brand.giveaway_official” with few posts and a recent creation date is not the brand.
  6. Requests for sensitive data. Bank logins, card numbers or passwords are never needed to deliver a prize.
  7. Links to unfamiliar domains. Hover before you click. Official winner forms live on the sponsor's domain or a known promotion platform.

How legitimate winner notification works

Real sponsors notify winners by the method stated in the official rules, usually email or phone, and may ask you to sign an affidavit of eligibility and a publicity/liability release. In the US, they may ask for a tax ID for prizes above reporting thresholds — on an official form, not in a chat message. If in doubt, contact the brand through its official website rather than replying to the message.

If you've been targeted

  • Do not reply or click links. Take screenshots.
  • Report the account on the platform and report fraud to your national consumer-protection agency (in the US, the FTC; in Canada, the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre).
  • If you shared payment details, call your bank immediately.
Simple test: If you must give money to get money, walk away.
